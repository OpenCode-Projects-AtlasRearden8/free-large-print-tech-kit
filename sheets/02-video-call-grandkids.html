Video Call the Grandkids

See their faces — it works like a regular call.

  1. Before your first call: ask someone to put the grandkids' names in two places on your phone — in Contacts and in the video app (FaceTime, WhatsApp, or Zoom). After that, calling is just two taps.
  2. To start a call: open Contacts, tap the name, then tap the video camera icon. The phone rings for them just like a normal call.
  3. When they call you: the phone rings and shows a video camera icon on a green circle. Tap it to answer. Their face, and yours, appear on screen together.
  4. Hold it steady: rest the phone against something at eye level, about an arm's length away. Facing a window is best — light on your face, room lit behind you.
  5. Sound troubles? Tap the speaker icon so the sound comes out loud. Hold the phone up when you talk, not down by your lap.
  6. Screen frozen? Press the red Hang Up and call again. The second call usually works fine.
  7. To end the call: press the big red circle. You will hear normal silence. That's all.
Remember: video calls use the internet, not phone minutes or a calling card. They cost nothing extra.
