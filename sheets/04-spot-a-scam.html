How to Spot a Scam

Five red flags. If you see one, slow down.

  • It's an emergency. “Last warning.” “Act right now.” Real companies and real family give you time to think.
  • Strange payment. Gift cards, wire transfers, crypto. Real offices send letters you can carry to the bank.
  • They ask for a PIN, code, or password. No real bank or company will ever ask you for these.
  • Government or “Microsoft” calls. The IRS does not call; nobody calls about your computer being broken.
  • “Keep this secret.” This is how they stop you from checking the story.

What to do, every time

  1. Hang up. Real people who need you will leave a message.
  2. Call the real number yourself — from a bill or your card, not the number they gave you.
  3. Never “press 1 to be removed” or say “yes” before you know who it is.
  4. Check, don't trust. For “grandchild” calls, call the grandchild or their parent on a known number.
Remember: nothing can happen to you from hanging up. Your bank and your family will tell you if it was real.
