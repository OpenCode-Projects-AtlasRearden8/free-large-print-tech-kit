Safe Habits on the Web

Just five habits. That's enough.

  1. Use bookmarks. Ask a family member to put big bookmark icons for your bank, doctor's site, and church on the home screen — then you never type an address again.
  2. Scary pop-up? Do not tap anything on it. Press Back to leave. Nothing breaks if you just close it.
  3. “Click here to verify” emails and texts are almost always fake. Go to the real site with your bookmark instead. Real companies never punish you for not clicking.
  4. Before typing a password or card number, check the address bar shows the site you expected and a closed lock. If the request came from a text or email, use your bookmark instead.
  5. Never sign up for anything that popped up on its own. Real sites want your attention; fake ones want your name, number, and card.
  6. Tell someone if something feels wrong — a strange message, an odd charge, a phone number you don't know. Asking is always faster than undoing.
If it looks scary: you cannot break the phone or the internet. Press Back, or restart the phone by holding the side button, then turning it back on. Nothing online can hurt you if you never type your password or card numbers into a pop-up.
